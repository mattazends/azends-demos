Roof types

Four kinds of roof. One crew.

Tucson has all of them, sometimes on the same house. Pick yours to see how it wears in the desert and what we do about it.

Tile · underlayment · deck

Concrete & clay tile

The tile is tough and lasts a long time. The underlayment beneath it generally lasts about eight to 20 years source, and even one cracked tile can let monsoon water slip underneath. So most tile jobs are a repair or a re-lay, not a whole new roof.

  • Cracked & slipped tiles Lifted, checked underneath, matched and reset
  • Underlayment replacement Tiles off and stacked, new layer down, tiles back
  • Valleys & flashing Where the water concentrates
  • Full tile re-lay New underlayment, battens and flashings
Get a tile roof estimate
Parapet · coating · foam · scupper

Flat, foam & built-up

Flat roofs with parapet walls are a classic Tucson look, and they aren't truly flat: they slope gently to scuppers or drains. A foam roof is only as good as its coating, and the guidance is to recoat every five to 10 years source. Caught in time, a recoat costs far less than replacing the foam.

  • Foam recoating Before the sun gets to the foam
  • Ponding & low spots Water that sits long after a storm
  • Parapet caps & scuppers Cracked caps, blocked outlets
  • Strip & replace When patching is past its limit
Get a flat roof estimate
Deck · underlayment · shingles

Asphalt shingle

Usually the most affordable roof to replace. A proper replacement means tearing off the old layers, checking and repairing the deck, new underlayment and edge metal, then new architectural shingles. Curling, cracked or bare shingles and granules in the gutters are the signs to watch.

  • Shingle repairs Missing, lifted or damaged sections
  • Rotted decking Cut out and replaced before anything goes over it
  • Full re-roof Tear-off to new architectural shingles
  • New construction Roofs for new builds and additions
Get a shingle roof estimate

Metal

What's actually up there

A tile roof is five layers. Most people only ever see one.

01/05
LAYER 01 · TILE

The part you see.

Concrete or clay. It takes the sun and sheds most of the rain, and it lasts for decades. But it is armour, not waterproofing.

Fails when
It cracks from foot traffic, branches or age, or slides out of line.
We do
Lift, check underneath, match and reset.
LAYER 02 · BATTENS

What the tile hangs on.

Strips fixed across the roof that the tiles sit against. When tiles keep slipping, the problem is often down here, not the tile itself.

Fails when
Fasteners let go or the wood breaks down.
We do
Replace battens as part of any re-lay.
LAYER 03 · UNDERLAYMENT

The one that matters.

This membrane is what actually protects the house. Heat and UV dry it out until it cracks and tears, which is why a roof can look fine from the street and still leak.

Lasts
Generally about eight to 20 years under tile. source
We do
Section repairs or a full replacement under your existing tile.
LAYER 04 · DECKING

The floor of the roof.

Sheets of wood everything else is fixed to. Once water gets past the underlayment, this is what rots. A soft or sagging spot underfoot is the giveaway.

Fails when
A leak goes unnoticed for a season or two.
We do
Cut out the rot and replace it before anything goes back on.
LAYER 05 · FRAMING

What carries it all.

Rafters and trusses. Most roofers stop above this line. We come from a family of general contractors, so if the structure needs work, the same crew does it.

Fails when
Long-term leaks or termites get into the wood.
We do
Structural repairs in-house, then rebuild the roof over it.
The Tucson roof year

Months of sun. Then it all comes at once.

0days until monsoon season

The quiet months

Oct to Mar

Dry, mild and easy to work in. Nothing is testing the roof, so small problems stay hidden. This is the time to find them.

  • Get the roof looked at once monsoon has passed
  • Clear debris from valleys, scuppers and flat roofs
  • Book re-roofs and underlayment work while it's dry

Peak sun

Apr to mid Jun

Week after week of heat and UV. This is what dries out underlayment and wears foam coatings thin. Last call before the rain.

  • Replace cracked and slipped tiles
  • Recoat foam roofs that are due
  • Check flashing, vents and parapet caps

Monsoon

Jun 15 to Sep 30

The National Weather Service in Tucson defines monsoon as June 15 through September 30 source. Heavy, wind-driven rain pushes water sideways under tiles and backs it up behind blocked scuppers.

  • Never go up on the roof during a storm
  • Catch the water, move what you can, take photos
  • Call us once it passes. A temporary cover may be possible
